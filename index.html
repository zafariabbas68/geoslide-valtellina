Slope angle

The single strongest predictor of landslide occurrence. As the angle increases, shear stress rises and the probability of failure grows — making slope the backbone of the model.

Circular · ° 03

Aspect direction

Slope direction controls solar exposure, snowmelt timing, and soil moisture — all of which shift the conditions that trigger shallow landslides.

Derived from DTM0–360°
Continuous · 1/m 04

Plan curvature

Highlights where water converges or diverges laterally. Concave plan positions collect runoff and raise pore-water pressure — a precondition for slope failure.

Derived from DTMContinuous
Continuous · 1/m 05

Profile curvature

Controls where material accelerates or decelerates downslope. Convex profiles are prone to detachment; concave zones tend to accumulate failed material.

Pipeline

From raw layers to risk-informed decisions

Three stages, ten predictors, one thousand sample points — validated end-to-end.

01

Data Preprocessing

Slope, aspect, plan & profile curvature derived from the DTM. Distance buffers to faults, roads, and rivers. No-Landslide Zones removed where they overlap the IFFI inventory.

02

Susceptibility Model

R + ModelMap with 700 training and 300 testing points. Continuous susceptibility raster (0–1), validated with an error matrix. Overall accuracy: 0.69.

03

Exposure Assessment

Susceptibility reclassified into low / moderate / high / very high. Zonal statistics with WorldPop reveal population counts per risk class.
